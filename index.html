Abstract

Non-prehensile manipulation enables robots to reposition objects through contact across diverse geometries and physical properties without relying on grasping. We study goal-directed arm-mediated object pushing with a quadrupedal mobile manipulator and propose a hierarchical reinforcement learning framework that decouples task-level decision making from low-level dynamic stabilization. The high-level policy outputs target base velocities and arm-joint targets. A pretrained low-level locomotion policy tracks the base command to produce dynamically stable locomotion, while a separate arm controller tracks the commanded joint targets. To guide exploration toward feasible robot-object interactions, we introduce geometry-guided contact sampling from the object point cloud and use the sampled target to shape end-effector reaching before shifting emphasis toward the object-pose objective. We evaluate performance and robustness across object families and distribution shifts. The learned policy remains effective under geometry and physics variation and generalizes to unseen everyday-object geometries. Ablations further show that contact guidance, the staged reward curriculum, and online adaptation are important for learning and transfer.

Method

The policy stack has two levels: a high-level goal-conditioned actor for task strategy and a frozen low-level locomotion prior for dynamic stability. The actor outputs base velocities together with arm-joint targets for arm-mediated pushing.

Real-World Deployment

As an initial step toward real-world transfer, we conduct a real-twin feasibility study on Spot by replicating the simulation task setup in the physical environment. We record the policy's high-level commands in simulation, including SE(2) base velocities and arm targets, and replay them on hardware in a matched setting while tracking the commands. This demonstrates that the task structure transfers to the robot and motivates future closed-loop onboard deployment.
